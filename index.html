Player auction – rules & guidelines

Monday, 16 November 2026 · 6:00 PM · GNC Conference Hall, First Floor

  1. 1
    Objectives

    Competitive bidding: Fair opportunities for owners and strategists to bid for preferred players. Purse discipline: Every team completes its squad within the notional purse. The computerised system manages eligibility, limits and calculations.

  2. 2
    Team Composition & Auction Purse

    Six teams; 16 players per team including the preselected playing owner and strategist. Each team acquires 14 players at auction. Category quota: A+ 2 | A 2 | B+ 5 | B 5 | WD 2. Total purse ₹11,40,000, less owner ₹70,000 and strategist ₹85,000, leaving ₹9,85,000 for auction.

  3. 3
    A+ Elite Restriction (within A+ category)

    Of the 12 A+ players, six are A+ Elite and six are Regular A+. Each team must finish with one A+ Elite and one Regular A+; no team may hold two Elite players. A team with a preselected Elite owner or strategist cannot bid for, recall or receive another Elite player. The software enforces this rule throughout bidding and allocation. A+ remains one official squad category.

  4. 4
    Auction Process & Bid Increments

    Players are provisionally scheduled in five computer-randomised batches, with the Elite/Regular A+ mix managed within the A+ count. Every player opens at ₹5,000. Bid increments are ₹5,000 through ₹50,000; ₹10,000 above ₹50,000 through ₹1,00,000; ₹20,000 thereafter. Teams may bid only for eligible vacancies and within their permissible capacity.

  5. 5
    Purse Protection

    The system protects ₹70,000 for every unfilled A+, A or B+ position, and ₹30,000 for every unfilled B or WD position. These are not opening or minimum sale prices. When bidding for a position, that position’s protection is released; all other vacancies remain protected.

    Purse protection – permissible biddingA team has ₹2,00,000 remaining and needs one A+ (₹70,000 protected), one B (₹30,000) and one WD (₹30,000). For A+, it may bid up to ₹1,40,000, retaining ₹60,000 for B and WD. For B or WD, the ceiling is ₹1,00,000, retaining ₹1,00,000 for the other two vacancies. Actual bids must follow increments. Protected amounts are not minimum bids.
    After purchasing a playerA team starts with ₹9,85,000, of which ₹7,00,000 is protected and ₹2,85,000 is free. It buys a B player for ₹50,000. Remaining purse becomes ₹9,35,000; protection falls to ₹6,70,000; free bidding capacity becomes ₹2,65,000.
  6. 6
    Unsold Players, Recall & Highest Valid Bid

    Each player not already allocated is offered once in regular bidding. Unsold players enter a recall round and are offered again. Every new bid must exceed the current highest bid by the required increment; matching bids are prohibited. If nobody places the next valid bid, the highest bidder wins. No competitive-bid lottery applies.

    Highest valid bid – no tied bidsTeam X bids ₹80,000. Team Y’s maximum permissible capacity is ₹85,000, so it cannot place the next valid bid of ₹90,000 or match ₹80,000. Team X wins unless another team bids higher.
  7. 7
    Automatic & Random Allocation

    A+, A and B+ (no surplus): When only one eligible team remains for a player or category vacancy, allocation may occur immediately at ₹70,000, including players scheduled in later batches. For A+, Elite eligibility and the one-Elite-per-team restriction always take precedence; the system must not allocate an Elite player to an ineligible team or create an impossible remaining Elite/Regular distribution. If multiple eligible teams remain, bidding continues; after regular offers and recall are exhausted, remaining players are randomly allocated among eligible vacancies at ₹70,000, subject to valid completion of all squads.

    B and WD (surplus): After competitive bidding and recall are exhausted, the system randomly selects and allocates players to remaining vacancies at ₹30,000; surplus players remain unselected. All allocations are mandatory, with immediate purse deduction and no owner choice, rejection or exchange.

    A+ automatic allocation and later batchesAfter the Batch 3 A+ Elite player is sold, the fourth auction-eligible Elite is automatically allocated at ₹70,000 to the sole team still without an Elite. The Batch 4 Elite entry therefore does not go to bidding. Likewise, the last Regular A+ and A players are auto-allocated when only one eligible team remains, normally after Batch 4.
    A and B+ – mandatory category allocationThere are 12 A players in total, including four preselected, leaving eight in the auction; and 30 B+ players, including three preselected, leaving 27 in the auction. If two teams each still need one A after six A players have been purchased, the two remaining A players are allocated at ₹70,000 each after all five batches and the recall round, if still unselected. The same principle applies to B+ at ₹70,000 per player.
    B – surplus category allocationThere are 43 B players in total: two preselected and 41 in the auction. Teams must buy 28 B players in aggregate. If three teams each still need one B after 25 B players have been purchased, 16 B players remain. After bidding in all five batches and the subsequent recall round, the system randomly allocates three of the 16 to eligible teams at ₹30,000 each; the remaining 13 are unselected.
    Women’s Doubles – surplus category allocationAll 15 WD players enter the auction, and teams need 12 in total. If two teams each still need one WD player after ten have been purchased, five WD players remain. After bidding in all five batches and the subsequent recall round, two are randomly allocated at ₹30,000 each; the remaining three are unselected.

  8. 8
    Auctioneer’s Call & Spirit of the Auction

    The auctioneer runs the room and the screen records it. If the auctioneer’s call and the computer entry ever differ, the auctioneer’s call is final and the screen is corrected.

    Player introductions, descriptions and banter during the auction are all in good fun, not a rating of anyone. Prices also reflect auction dynamics: each team is balancing its player mix, categories and purse at that moment, so a bid says more about a team’s needs than about a player’s ability.

  9. 9
    General Rules

    The system enforces category quotas, Elite eligibility, bid increments, purse protection and allocations; no negative balance is allowed. A reconciliation break verifies squads, purse and unsold pool before final completion. The Tournament Committee handles exceptions fairly.

    All auction values are purely notional for club team selection; no actual monetary transactions are involved.
